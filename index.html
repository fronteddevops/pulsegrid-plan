[ 00 ] EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

A category-defining bet on continuous, contextual care.

Healthcare today is episodic. A patient sees a clinician for 12 minutes, then disappears for weeks. PulseGrid closes that gap with a stack that lives where the patient lives — at the bedside, on the wrist, on the wall — feeding a single platform that the hospital, the lab, the old-age home, the family, and the patient all share. The differentiator is not another EMR; it is the data layer underneath the EMR. Sensors that don't sleep. Workflows that don't depend on a stretched-thin nurse remembering to walk in.

[ 01 ] PROBLEM

Care happens in the gaps. Today, no one is watching the gaps.

⏱

Episodic, not continuous

A vital sign is taken once a shift on a paper chart. Deterioration that begins at 2 AM is found at the 6 AM round — often six hours too late.

🧩

Fragmented data silos

The hospital, the lab, the family doctor, and the old-age home each hold a slice. The patient stitches them together with a folder of photocopies.

⚠

Elderly fall risk goes unseen

Falls are the leading cause of injury death in the over-65 group. Most happen at night, in a room where no one is looking.

↻

Avoidable readmissions

A meaningful share of 30-day hospital readmissions are predictable from at-home vitals — but that data is never collected.

☺

Sleep and recovery are invisible

Recovery quality is largely a function of sleep, restlessness, and night-time movement. None of it is measured outside a sleep lab.

☎

Family is locked out

Adult children paying for elderly care have no objective view of how their parent is doing — only a phone call, only when something is already wrong.

[ 03 ] THE USP

The Smart Care Room — our defensible moat.

Software platforms are easy to copy. A bedside hardware footprint, validated through clinical pilots and certified for medical use, is not. PulseGrid's edge is that we ship the room itself.

  • Continuous, ambient sensing — vitals captured by mmWave radar without touching the patient, wearables only when the clinical use case demands it.
  • Privacy-first design — radar over cameras wherever possible; cameras only with explicit consent and on-device blur.
  • Edge AI on the bedside hub — fall detection, NEWS2 scoring, and sleep staging run locally; the network is for sync, not for safety.
  • Modular kit — ICU rooms get the full stack; old-age home rooms get a lighter set; a home-care kit fits in a shoebox.

Offline-first safety contract

If the cloud is unreachable, the bedside hub continues to: (a) capture and locally store every reading for up to 7 days, (b) run NEWS2 + fall detection with full alerting on the in-room display and pendant buzzer, (c) trigger LTE failover for critical alerts only. When connectivity returns, all data syncs in the original timestamp order.

[ 10 ] AI / ML PIPELINE

Where the models live, and what they protect against.

A

Ingest & fuse

Per-second vitals + pressure mat + radar streams enter the hub. Sensor fusion produces a 1 Hz unified frame: HR, SpO₂, breath, restlessness, presence, posture.

B

Edge models

Fall classifier, NEWS2 scorer, sleep stager run on the hub. Latency < 1 s for fall alerts; never depends on the network.

C

Cloud models

24-hour rolling windows feed deterioration prediction, adherence anomaly, and personalised baselines. Re-trained weekly.

D

Decision routing

Critical: pendant buzzer + nurse station + family push, all simultaneously. Non-critical: daily digest. Predictive: clinician task queue.

E

Clinician copilot

Phase 3 LLM summarises a patient's last 24 h in two sentences for handoff and rounding, with citations back into the raw timeline.

[ 18 ] WHY NOW

A rare alignment of demographics, infrastructure, and silicon.

Aging populations everywhere

India's over-60 cohort doubles by 2050. The EU passes 30% over-65 by mid-century. The US adds 25M over-65 by 2040. The UK NHS faces a chronic-care crisis today. The maths is the same in every market: not enough nurses, not enough beds.

National health data fabrics are real

India's ABDM, the UK's NHS Digital, the US's Carequality network, the EU's European Health Data Space — the public-sector health-data plumbing that was missing for a decade is now real infrastructure. Building on it is no longer a bet.

Post-COVID home-care normalisation

Patients and families now expect to be monitored at home. The objection isn't comfort — it's the absence of a credible product.

Sensor & edge-AI cost collapse

60 GHz radar, BLE 5 SoCs, and edge TPUs are at a price point that didn't exist three years ago. The bedside hub is finally affordable per room.

Insurance is hungry for prevention

Indian and global health insurers are actively buying telemetry that lowers their loss ratios — we ship exactly that telemetry.

The EMR layer alone has lost

EMRs digitised the chart. They didn't continuously watch the patient. The next decade in health tech belongs to the layer that does.

[ 19 ] COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

Adjacent giants. None of them are us.

There are leaders in patient monitoring, in single-condition wearables, and in aging-in-place. None of them ship a multi-stakeholder, multi-condition, multi-market platform that runs end-to-end from the bedside to the family's phone.

Hospital incumbents

Philips IntelliVue · GE CARESCAPE · Mindray

Trusted in ICU, deep clinical integration, certified for high-acuity care. Sell $10K+ monitor towers per bed. Strong in US, EU, India, China.

How we differ: ~1/10th the cost per room, modular kit, follows the patient home. The incumbents stop at the hospital door — we keep going.

Continuous-care startup

Biofourmis · Current Health

FDA-cleared continuous monitoring + AI for cardiology and post-acute. Strong US enterprise traction; Current Health was acquired by Best Buy in 2021.

How we differ: Multi-modal sensing (mat + radar + wearable, not wearable-only), multi-stakeholder platform, multi-market regulatory from day one.

CGM leaders

Abbott FreeStyle Libre · Dexcom

Gold-standard CGM. Mass-market diabetic users, mature regulatory, billions in annual revenue. Available across all major markets.

How we differ: CGM is one sensor in our 13-device stack. Glucose + sleep + HR + falls + meds in a single platform — not a single-condition silo.

ECG patch leaders

iRhythm Zio · Bardy DX

Best-in-class arrhythmia detection, FDA cleared, distributed via cardiologists. Strong post-event analysis with 14-day patches.

How we differ: Real-time alerting (not retrospective), integrated with vitals, sleep, falls, meds. Cardiac is one of many use cases — not the entire product.

TeleCare · aging-in-place

Tunstall · Vayyar Care · Lively

Established in UK and EU TeleCare; US elder-care via Best Buy. Radar-based fall detection, pendant alarms, basic vitals. Tunstall has 5M+ connected users globally.

How we differ: Continuous clinical-grade vitals (not just falls), hospital integration, lab connectivity. We're upstream from the alarm.

Consumer wearables

Apple Watch · Fitbit · Samsung Health

Massive distribution, expanding clinical features (AFib, fall detection, sleep apnea). Strong UX, broad consumer trust.

How we differ: Hospital-grade and clinically accountable. The watch becomes a sensor in our network, not the network itself. Episodic vs. continuous.
